[excited] What if every shooting star landed?

Twenty five million hit our air every single day. But that's not even the craziest part.

Most are smaller than a grain of sand. They slam in at up to seventy two kilometres every second.

Each one becomes a tiny fireball. The air in front of them gets squeezed so hard, it glows at over sixteen hundred degrees... and they vaporise nearly a hundred kilometres up. Gone.

Now take the air away. Want to see what happens? Look at the Moon. No air... so every speck hits. Nothing slows them down. Its whole surface gets slowly sandblasted, forever.

[dramatic] But here's the crazy part. Some shooting stars already land! Today. The tiniest bits slow down, cool off, and drift down as dust. About five thousand tons of it every year. There's probably stardust on your roof right now.
